# OASIS Planetary Climate Physics

## Building virtual worlds that follow universal laws of physics

What makes a planet warm, cold, habitable—or completely unlike anything in our Solar System?

At the University of Southampton, we develop **OASIS**: a connected family of scientific models for exploring how planetary atmospheres move, evolve and interact with radiation, surfaces and observations.

Our goal is to build virtual planetary worlds grounded in physics. By connecting climate simulations with the light that planets emit or reflect, we aim to help researchers understand both familiar planets and distant worlds that cannot be visited directly.

## From physical laws to observable worlds

```text
Molecular physics
       ↓
Atmospheric heating and cooling
       ↓
One- and three-dimensional climates
       ↓
Spectra and model libraries
       ↓
Comparison with observations
```

OASIS brings these stages together while allowing researchers to use only the parts they need.

## The OASIS ecosystem

| Component | What it helps us understand |
|---|---|
| **OASIS** | How atmospheres circulate and climates emerge across a three-dimensional planet. |
| **OASIS-1D** | How atmospheric structure, radiation and convection interact in a single atmospheric column. |
| **OASIS-XS** | How molecules absorb and emit light—the radiative building blocks used by the climate models. |
| **OASIS-Retriever** | What observations can tell us about an atmosphere when compared with simulated spectra and model libraries. |

Together, these tools connect fundamental molecular data to planetary-scale climates and, ultimately, to what telescopes can observe.

## Foundation

This work is being advanced through **Foundation — “Building Virtual Worlds that Follow Universal Laws of Physics”**, a University of Southampton research programme supported by the Horizon Europe Guarantee (EP/Z00330X/1, September 2024–September 2029).

Foundation develops the science and collaborations behind the project. OASIS is the software ecosystem through which that research is implemented and shared.
